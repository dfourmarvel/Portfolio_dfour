Why Nigerian scammers say they are from Nigeria

Sep 04, 2026 social engineering from linkedin

There's an academic paper literally titled "Why Do Nigerian Scammers Say They Are From Nigeria?" Cormac Herley wrote it at Microsoft Research in 2012, and it undid an assumption I've been carrying around for years.

I'd always filed the bad grammar under non-native authorship, someone running a scam from a country where English wasn't the first language, working off a script nobody had properly translated. And I assumed AI would quietly close that gap. Better translation, then generative models, smoothing the language barrier until the emails read clean. If that were the whole story, phishing should be getting harder to spot every year.

It isn't the whole story. Herley's argument is that the bad grammar is deliberate. Running a phishing campaign to completion is expensive: you have to build rapport, keep the story consistent, and walk someone through wiring money or handing over credentials, all without them getting suspicious partway through and reporting you. A scammer with limited time doesn't want a large pool of sceptical replies to work through. They want a small pool of people already inclined to believe them. The typos aren't a bug, they're user onboarding for a very specific type of user, and everyone who'd eventually catch on gets screened out before the scammer spends a minute on them.

Non-native authorship is still real, a lot of this infrastructure does run out of regions where English isn't the operator's first language. But it's a secondary cause sitting on top of something intentional, not the explanation itself.

Here's the part that actually unsettles me. The barrier I assumed AI would erase, it already has, just not for the messages I was picturing. Business email compromise and spear-phishing, the ones built around impersonating an executive or targeting one specific person, are increasingly AI-generated and read cleanly, because the target is worth the extra production cost. The volume scams stay deliberately rough on purpose. The targeted ones got fluent because someone decided you were worth the effort.
