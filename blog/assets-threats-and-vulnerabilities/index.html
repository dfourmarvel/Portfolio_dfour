Three modules in, my laptop stopped turning on

Jul 15, 2026 coursework from linkedin

Three modules into Assets, Threats, and Vulnerabilities, my laptop stopped turning on. The recent floods got to it before I could finish the section on how organisations protect what they value, which is not the kind of coincidence you want when the entire course is about exactly that problem.

This is course five of the Google Cybersecurity Certificate, and it's the one I stalled on longest. The first four courses were technical in a way that gave immediate feedback: run a Linux command, write a SQL query, watch it work or fail. This one is slower. It's about classifying what an organisation actually has worth protecting, applying the NIST risk management framework to decide how much that protection is worth spending on, and using MITRE's CVE system, a public catalogue that assigns every known vulnerability its own name and number before anyone gets to exploit it quietly. There's no green checkmark for "correctly assessed risk." You just have to reason it through.

The back half covers the threat side: social engineering, malware, web-based exploits, and how security teams build threat models to think like the person trying to get in before that person shows up. It reframed vulnerability management for me, less as a checklist and more as a constant, unfinished argument between what you can afford to protect and what someone else has decided is worth attacking.

Between the laptop going in for repair and a course module that never really grabbed me the way the earlier, hands-on courses did, this one took longer than any course before it. Something is fitting about needing a repair before I could finish a course on protecting what you value. I noticed the irony. I did not enjoy it.
