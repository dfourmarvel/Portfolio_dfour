I spent yesterday telling strangers blood donation was safe

Jul 15, 2026 community health from linkedin

I spent yesterday telling strangers blood donation was safe. Today I found out I hadn't fully convinced myself.

As part of the team from Entrance University of Health Sciences, we'd spent yesterday reaching out to the communities around us, Spintex, Manet, and the surrounding areas, knocking on doors and stopping people on the street to talk about the Blood Donation and Health Screening Day happening today. A lot of them had the same doubts, that it would leave them weak, that something could go wrong, that it just wasn't worth the risk. I answered every one of those doubts with confidence.

Then today, I sat down for my own turn, and I was the first person in the chair. No one ahead of me to watch go through it fine, no reassurance from seeing it work for someone else first, just me and everything I'd told other people the day before.

The needle went in. It was quick, and honestly a bit anticlimactic given how much I'd built it up in my head over the last ten minutes. What stuck with me wasn't the process, it was the gap between what I knew and what I felt. I could recite the physiology. I still needed a minute to actually believe it applied to me.

By the time it was done, the nervousness felt almost embarrassing in hindsight, given how many people I'd talked into doing the exact same thing the day before. But maybe that's the honest version of the story. Telling someone it's safe is easy. Finding out you believed yourself is a different thing entirely.
