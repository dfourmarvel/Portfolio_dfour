TB is preventable. TB is curable. But only if it's taken seriously.

Mar 24, 2026 health awareness from linkedin

On World Tuberculosis Day, it's worth revisiting a disease many assume is "under control" — but isn't.

Tuberculosis (TB) remains one of the world's deadliest infectious diseases, and its impact is still very real in Ghana.

Recent data shows:

  • Ghana records ~20,000+ TB cases annually, yet thousands more go undiagnosed
  • Treatment success rates are high (over 90%), proving TB is curable when detected early
  • A significant number of cases — especially in children — are still missed each year

TB spreads through the air when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or even talks. The early signs are often ignored:

  • Persistent cough
  • Weight loss
  • Chest pain
  • Fatigue

The key issue isn't just treatment — it's late detection and low awareness.

What can be done:

  • Seek medical attention for any cough lasting more than 2 weeks
  • Take advantage of free TB testing available in Ghana
  • Complete the full course of treatment if diagnosed
  • Reduce stigma — it delays people from seeking care

TB is preventable. TB is curable. But only if it's taken seriously.

How many people around us might be ignoring the early signs?
