The queue for finger pricks never got shorter

Jul 15, 2026 community health from linkedin

The queue for finger pricks never got shorter. It just changed faces.

At today's screening, my station was pricking fingers, running blood type, hepatitis B, blood sugar, malaria, and typhoid tests off a single drop, one person after another, for hours. As soon as one person walked off nursing a cotton ball, the next was already sitting down. There's a particular kind of pressure in doing something precise, sharp, and slightly painful to a stranger, over and over, at a pace that doesn't leave room to think too hard about any single one of them.

Some of the people in that queue were children. A few cried before the lancet even touched their skin. I still had to do the same thing to them that I'd just done to the adult before, hold the finger steady, get a clean sample first try, keep moving, because there were still forty more people behind them.

What surprised me wasn't the technical part. That stayed easy the whole day, positioning, pressure, angle, done. What took actual effort was doing it well while a child looked at me like I was about to hurt them, and not letting that show up in my hands. Composure under repetition is one thing. Composure while someone is actively upset with you for something you know is necessary is a different skill entirely, and nobody teaches it in a lecture hall.

By the time the queue ended, it had stopped feeling remarkable at all. That's probably the most honest thing I can say about it.
