Talking to strangers about blood donation

Jul 14, 2026 community health from linkedin

Today I did something that doesn't come naturally to me. I walked up to strangers, again and again, and started conversations.

As part of our SRC celebrations, our team went out into the community to talk about blood donation ahead of tomorrow's Blood Donation and Health Screening Day at Entrance University of Health Sciences, in collaboration with the National Blood Service. Most of the people we met had questions rooted in fear more than fact. Some thought donating blood would make them weak or sick. Others weren't sure it was safe at all. We sat with them, explained the actual process, and watched the hesitation turn into interest.

Blood donation was only part of what we were inviting them to. Tomorrow's event also offers free screening for malaria, blood pressure, and blood type, along with free consultations. For a lot of people we spoke to, that alone was worth showing up for, even before they'd fully warmed to the idea of donating.

I'm introverted by nature. Approaching people I don't know isn't where I'm comfortable. But something about believing in what I was saying made it easier. I wasn't selling anything. I was just telling people the truth: this is simple, it's safe, and it could catch something that matters before it becomes serious. I'll also be on the team running the screening itself tomorrow, so I get to see it through from the invitation to the checkup.

The screening happens tomorrow. If today was any indication, we're going to see people who walked past us skeptical this morning show up ready to give, get checked, or both.
