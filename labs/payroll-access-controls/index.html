Payroll Incident: Access Controls Worksheet

A formal authorization/authentication worksheet tracing a fraudulent payroll transfer to systemic over-privileging.

Event log analysisRBACPrinciple of least privilegeAccount lifecycle management

A payroll transfer went to an unknown account. The formal access-controls worksheet below documents the event, the root-cause issue, and the recommended remediation — the structured deliverable behind the incident narrative.

The event

Category: Authorization / Authentication. Caused by Legal staff, occurring 10/03/2023 at 8:29:57 AM from a device flagged Up2-NoGud.

The underlying issue

The company operates under a highly vulnerable access model where administrative privileges are universally granted to all personnel — including part-time workers and external contractors — regardless of role. This severe over-privileging is compounded by a total failure in account lifecycle management: a contractor whose contract expired years earlier retained active administrative access to critical infrastructure.

Recommendation

  • Enforce the Principle of Least Privilege — revoke administrative rights from all standard users, contractors, and part-time staff, granting only the minimum access their role requires.
  • Transition the network architecture to a Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) framework, tying permissions to documented business functions rather than convenience.
  • Establish strict identity-management protocols that mandate immediate disabling of temporary, part-time, and contract accounts the day employment ends.
  • Conduct a full security audit of remaining administrative accounts, rotate credentials immediately, and enforce MFA enterprise-wide.

root cause

Not a technical exploit — a ghost account. A contract that ended in 2019 still carried live admin access in 2023, inside an organization where every role, regardless of seniority, was already over-privileged by default. The fix is organizational discipline, not a patch.
